# Voice and tone

The learnings from tuning investor-facing prose. Two principles pull against
each other; balance them:

- Be **punchy and plain** (the "Y Combinator / founder" register): short
  sentences, present tense, bold but honest confidence.
- **Reduce theatricality.** AI- and startup-speak overuse rhetorical drama.
  A restrained, direct read reads *more* confident than a breathless one.

## For the war-room analogy

For a decision product, frame it as **a virtual team that debates every angle
and then lands on a conclusion** — opposition, deliberation, verdict — not as
"Perplexity for X" (a search-answer framing that cheapens a deliberation
product). Tie the analogy back to the brand name where possible (e.g.,
*counterpoint* = a second melody arguing with the first).

## Cut the theatrical patterns

Apply the clear-writing rules (separate skill) aggressively to an investor
draft. The patterns that most often creep in:

- **"— because" after an em-dash.** Split into two sentences. Don't start the
  second clause with "because"; that reads deliberately staged.
  - Bad: "the team approves it, then everyone commits — because the room looks
    good and nobody asked the question yet."
  - Good: "The team approves it. Then everyone commits. The room looks good,
    and nobody has asked the question yet."
- **"Not X. It's Y."** Theatrical contrast. If the contrast is genuinely
  needed, state it flatly ("The technology exists. The process does not.").
  Otherwise write the plain point once.
- **The "It's not … this is the point" turn.** State the claim; cut the
  punch-line beat.
- **Em-dash chains and comma asides.** Prefer separate sentences.

## Preserve

Meaning, technical precision, logical validity, uncertainty, and the author's
voice. Only remove rhetorical noise. For a whitepaper, **branch specifics and
engineering move to the appendix** — do not strip the *claim* of its evidence.

## Helpful phrasings

- "decide on a good-feeling plan, find the miss late, rework" (the before).
- "run it through the engine, catch the blind spot before you commit" (after).
- The 10x: "a single avoided mistake in one big call can pay for a year of
  the tool."
- "The technology exists. The process does not."

## A word on cautious honesty

For investor-facing figures, hedge *explicitly* and guide the reader with
labels: "figures are model assumptions," "this is a shape, not a number." Name
the assumptions that matter and the uncertainty who can resolve them. This is
authenticity, not weasel-wording.
